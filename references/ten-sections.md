## 本文（動画から書き起こし・英語原文）
### 1 Hook
"My doctor added a third blood pressure medication and I finally asked: when does this end?"
### 2 Story/Scene
I was sitting in the exam room last month when Dr. Stevens pulled up my chart.
"Your pressure's still running high. 158 over 94. We're going to add Metoprolol to your regimen."
I nodded. Like I always do.
He was already typing the prescription when something made me ask:
"When do I get to stop taking these?"
He stopped typing. Looked at me.
"Stop taking them?"
"Yeah. When does my blood pressure... fix itself?"
The look on his face told me everything.
"Mr. Mitchell, hypertension is a chronic condition. These medications manage it. You'll need to stay on them."
"For how long?"
Silence.
Then: "Indefinitely."
That word hung in the air like a death sentence.
### 3 Credibility
I'm 62 years old.
I run four times a week. I eat oatmeal for breakfast. I haven't smoked in 30 years.
Three years ago, my doctor put me on Lisinopril because my BP was 168/96.
It brought my numbers down to around 155/90.
A year later, he added Amlodipine because "we need to get it lower."
Now Metoprolol.
Three medications. And my pressure is still 158/94.
Higher than when I started on one pill.
### 4 Rock Bottom
That night, I couldn't sleep.
Not because I was worried about my blood pressure.
Because I was doing math.
I pulled out all three prescription bottles and laid them on the kitchen table.
Linda came downstairs at 2:15 AM and found me sitting there with a calculator.
"What are you doing?"
"Calculating how much money I'm going to spend on these for the rest of my life."
Three prescriptions. $47/month after insurance.
Times 12 months.
Times 20 years if I'm lucky.
$11,280.
"And that's if they don't add more," I said. "Which they will. Because that's apparently how this works."
### 5 Problem Mechanism
I found a research paper from UCLA.
The title made my stomach drop: "Long-term ACE Inhibitor Use and Progressive Arterial Stiffness: A 10-Year Longitudinal Study."
Blood pressure medications work by forcing your arteries to relax.
They don't heal anything. They don't fix anything.
They just force the pressure down chemically.
But here's what nobody tells you:
When you force your arteries to relax with drugs, they stop doing it on their own.
The natural mechanisms shut off.
Your body forgets how.
It's like using a crutch for so long that your leg muscles atrophy.
Eventually, you can't walk without the crutch anymore.
The researcher wrote one sentence that I must have read ten times:
"Current hypertension protocols may inadvertently create the progressive dependency they seek to manage."
Translation: The medications were designed to trap you.
### 6 Solution Mechanism
They'd been studying elderly men in remote coastal villages.
Men in their 80s who had arterial flexibility comparable to American men in their 40s.
No medications. No prescriptions. No hypertension.
It was something they'd been consuming daily for their entire lives.
Aged garlic extract.
This wasn't regular garlic.
These villages had a 2,000-year tradition of aging garlic in ceramic crocks for exactly 24 months.
Raw garlic contains allicin—harsh sulfur compounds that get destroyed by stomach acid.
That's why garlic pills don't work.
But 24-month fermentation converts allicin into something called S-allylcysteine.
This compound has 98% bioavailability.
It doesn't force your arteries to relax.
It's like physical therapy for your cardiovascular system.
Medications hold your arteries in a relaxed position 24/7—like propping a door open until the hinges rust.
This compound teaches your arteries to flex naturally again—like oiling the hinges so the door opens and closes on its own.
### 7 Transformation
I started taking it on a Tuesday.
Didn't stop my medications yet. Just added it.
Week one: Nothing. BP still 158/94.
Week two: 154/91. Could be normal variation.
Week three: 149/87.
I started paying attention.
Week five: 142/84.
Week seven: 136/81.
Lower than it had been on three medications.
That's when I did something probably stupid.
I stopped taking the Metoprolol.
Week eight: 138/82. Still good.
Week nine: I stopped the Amlodipine.
Week ten: 141/83.
Week twelve: I stopped the Lisinopril.
Week thirteen: 139/80.
### 8 Victory
My appointment with Dr. Stevens was last week.
He pulled up my chart and his eyebrows went up.
"Your home readings have been excellent. The Metoprolol is working well."
"I'm not taking the Metoprolol," I said.
He looked at me.
"I'm not taking any of them. Haven't for six weeks."
His face went from confused to angry in about two seconds.
"Mr. Mitchell, that's extremely dangerous. You can't just stop—"
"My pressure is 136 over 79," I said. "Lower than it's been in three years. On three medications it was 158 over 94."
Silence.
"What are you taking instead?"
I told him about the aged garlic extract. The Stanford study. The arterial flexibility research.
He pulled it up on his computer.
Read for maybe two minutes.
Then: "I'm not familiar with this."
"I know," I said. "That's exactly the problem."
### 9 Identity Shift
It's been four months now.
My blood pressure this morning was 133/77.
No medications. No side effects. No progressive dosing.
But here's what really changed:
I don't feel trapped anymore.
Every morning I used to look at that pill organizer and think: This is forever.
Now I wake up and think: I'm free.
Linda says I'm different. "Lighter," she says.
She looked at me last week and said something that hit me hard:

Because I am.
My arteries are literally getting younger.
My last test showed arterial age reversed by 14 years.
Not just managed. Not just controlled.
Reversed.

## 本人の設計コメント（Canva右カラム・要旨）
- 市場は全ての主張を聞き飽きている→商品も約束も出さず「全員が生きた瞬間」を描く。内なる独白を言い当てると即信用。
- リードは会話の往復＝読者が頭の中で映像化する。売らない。
- この市場は怠け者ではない。「全部やったのに」を肯定して罪悪感を消す。
- 金額（$11,280）は研究で見えた2次欲求。痛みを増幅。
- 解決を売る前に「なぜ他が全部失敗したか」を機序で説明。責任を本人から「システム」へ移す。既に陰謀論寄りなので刺さる。
- 「ニンニクは試した」→効かなかった理由（胃酸でアリシン破壊）を与え「今回は違う」論理的理由を作る。
- 1週目は変化なし＝現実感。薬を1つずつ減らす＝彼らが夢見て信じなかった事。
- 医者の顔が困惑→怒りに変わる場面が「私が見つけた」の復讐。「That's exactly the problem」がフィニッシャー。
- 買っているのはサプリでなく自由・アイデンティティの回復。
- CTAは「買え」でなく挑戦。「I can't tell you what to do」＝逆心理。「never going back」は読者もしたいコミットメント。
- 1000超コメントの多くが「私の人生そのもの」。深いリサーチの成果。
