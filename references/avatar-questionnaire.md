SECTION 6: CURIOSITY, CONSPIRACY & FORGOTTEN HISTORY
💡 Schwartz: “When direct methods have failed, the copy writer must exploit hidden desires, resentments, and unvoiced protests.” Curiosity is the bridge between a tuned-out market and a sale. These questions uncover the conspiratorial, the forgotten, and the suppressed—the stories that make your prospect lean in.
6.1 — Unique Past Attempts

WHY? → What made that approach different from the mainstream solutions?
WHY? → What was the result? Did it work? Did it fail? Was it suppressed?
WHY? → If it worked, why isn’t everyone doing it? What’s the story behind its disappearance?
ANSWER: _______________________________________________
Is there a conspiratorial or suppressed story behind why older solutions didn’t get adoption?
WHY? → Who benefited from the old solution NOT working? (Pharma? Industry? Incumbents?)
WHY? → Would your prospect believe this story, or does it feel too tinfoil-hat for them?
WHY? → What’s the version of this story that feels credible and validating without sounding paranoid?

WHY? → Why were they forgotten? (New technology replaced them? Industry moved on? War interrupted?)
WHY? → Can you resurrect this story as a “rediscovered wisdom” narrative for your mechanism?

SECTION 7: UNIQUE MECHANISM (RMBC FRAMEWORK)
💡 RMBC: “The unique mechanism explains why your prospect is struggling AND how they can overcome it. By showing the mechanism behind the PROBLEM, you get the prospect to understand why they’ve failed. By showing the mechanism behind the SOLUTION, you get them to believe your product will succeed where everything else failed.”
7.1 — Mechanism Behind the Problem
What is the REAL reason your prospect’s problem exists? (Not the obvious answer—the hidden one.)
WHY? → Why haven’t they heard this explanation before?
WHY? → If you told them this, would it feel like a revelation or an accusation?
WHY? → Does this explanation remove blame from them personally? (It should.)
ANSWER: _______________________________________________
Why has everything they’ve tried before failed? What was the COMMON flaw in all previous solutions?
WHY? → Can you name that flaw with a simple, memorable phrase?
WHY? → Does identifying this flaw retroactively make sense of their entire history of failure?
WHY? → When they hear this explanation, do they think “I’M not broken—the solutions were”?
ANSWER: _______________________________________________
7.2 — Mechanism Behind the Solution
What is the unique mechanism in your product that solves the problem?
WHY? → Why does this mechanism work where others don’t?
WHY? → Can you explain it in one sentence a 12-year-old would understand?
WHY? → Does the mechanism sound new but logical—not magical or too-good-to-be-true?
ANSWER: _______________________________________________
What is the NAME of your mechanism? (Every great product has a named mechanism.)
WHY? → Why does naming it make it more believable?
WHY? → Does the name itself imply HOW it works?
WHY? → Can a customer repeat this name to a friend and have it make sense?
